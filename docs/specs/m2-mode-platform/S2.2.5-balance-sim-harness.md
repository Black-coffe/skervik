# S2.2.5 — Balance-simulation harness

## Why this story exists

E2.2 shipped four catch-up mechanics — friendly robber, robin-hood / poverty tokens,
final round + hidden VP, catch-up event tiles. **Every one of them is `false` on every
shipping preset.** `BALANCED_PROFILE` is literally `{...CLASSIC_PROFILE, randomness:
'balanced_deck'}` (`ruleProfile.ts:399-404`); `BLITZ_PROFILE` is Classic with `vpToWin: 8`
and tighter timers (`ruleProfile.ts:413-429`).

The M2 gate says "Classic / Balanced / Blitz all playable". Before **S2.5.4 (Lobby UI)**
lets a human select a profile, we must decide **what each preset actually is**. That
decision is a product decision, and the owner will make it (S2.2.6) — but it must be made
on **evidence**, not taste. This story produces the evidence.

**This story changes NO preset and NO rule.** It only makes it possible to *measure* them.

## Goal

`@skervik/bots` can simulate bot-vs-bot matches under **any registered rule profile**, and
a sweep script reports, per profile: **match length**, **comeback rate**, **final VP gap**,
and **per-seat win rate**.

## The three metrics (and why each)

| Metric | Definition | What decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| **Match length** | `SimResult.turns` — distribution (median, p90, max) | Does Blitz's `vpToWin: 8` actually shorten a match, or is the ≤60-min ceiling held only by timers? Feeds S2.1.3's adaptive calculator. |
| **Comeback rate** | Share of matches where the player **last by public VP at the midpoint turn** wins | The direct measure of whether catch-up cures *runaway leader* — one of the two chronic pains the product exists to design out. Classic is the baseline; each flag is a **delta** against it. |
| **Final VP gap** | `winnerVP − lastPlaceVP` at `phase:'finished'` (public VP) | Guards against a catch-up flag that raises comeback rate by inflating variance (a blowout in the other direction is not an improvement). |
| **Per-seat win rate** | Win share by seat index | Detects first-player advantage; also a sanity check that the sim isn't biased. Not a preset lever — a validity check on the sim itself. |

**Public VP only, everywhere.** Under `hiddenVp` the public value is what a player can
actually see, so measuring on public VP keeps the metric comparable across profiles. Never
read hidden VP for these metrics.

## Hard constraints (the landmines)

1. **Classic must stay byte-frozen.** `reduce.ts:143-172` sets `state.profileId` *only if
   the `match.started` event carries it* — precisely so pre-S2.1.1 golden fixtures replay
   identically. Therefore, when the sim runs Classic, `match.started` must be emitted
   **without a `profileId` key** (not `profileId: 'classic'`, not `profileId: undefined`).
   Otherwise the event bytes shift.
   *Forcing check:* a test asserts `'profileId' in matchStartedEvent === false` for the
   default/Classic path, and `=== true` for a non-Classic profile.

2. **The existing seed-sweep must stay green, unchanged.** `harness.test.ts:109-153`
   (24 seeds × 3 difficulties) is the no-stall gate. It must pass without edits — proving
   the `profileId` param is genuinely optional and Classic behavior is untouched.

3. **The flag must actually fire.** A measured "no effect" is worthless if the mechanic
   never triggered. *Forcing check:* over the `robinHoodTest` sweep, assert at least one
   `poverty.tokensGranted` event was emitted; over `eventTilesTest`, assert
   `finalState.sevensRolled` is defined and > 0; over `finalRoundTest`, assert at least one
   `game.finalRoundStarted`; over `balanced`, assert `finalState.balancedRollsDrawn > 0`.
   These four assertions are what separate a real measurement from a green no-op.

4. **Determinism / paired comparison.** Bot seeds are derived per `(difficulty, matchSeed,
   playerId)` (`harness.ts`) and are **independent of the profile**. Keep it that way: the
   same match seed must produce the same bot noise across all ten profiles, so profile
   deltas are paired, not confounded by resampled bot behavior. The sim must never feed the
   match seed to a bot (`Bot.decide` takes exactly `(state, playerId)` — `bot.ts:20-25`).

5. **Timers are inert in the sim.** `simulateMatch` caps by action count (`maxTurns`,
   default 6000), not wall clock. Blitz's tighter timers therefore have **zero** effect
   here; only `vpToWin: 8` is measurable. The report must state this explicitly — otherwise
   a reader will conclude Blitz's timer settings were validated when they were not.

6. **No wall-clock, no ambient RNG anywhere near core.** The sim lives in `bots`; core stays
   pure. `check-core-no-runtime-deps.mjs` must stay green.

7. The sweep is **long-running — it must NOT join `pnpm -r test`.** It is a `sim` script.
   Only the small forcing tests (Constraints 1–3) run in the test suite, at low seed counts.

## Sim design

- **Fixed cohort:** 4 players, all seats `difficulty: 'hard'`. Uniform strength isolates the
  *rule's* effect from bot-skill asymmetry. (Mixed-difficulty cohorts are a later question;
  do not add them now.)
- **Seeds:** default 100 per profile, `--seeds N` overridable. Derive them deterministically
  (the `deriveValue('sweep-salt', i)` pattern at `harness.test.ts:113-125`) so any run is
  reproducible and every profile sees the **same seed set**.
- **Midpoint VP:** `SimResult` exposes only `finalState` + `events` (`harness.ts:29-34`) —
  no per-turn snapshots. Do **not** extend `SimResult` with snapshots. Instead fold the
  returned `events` with core's `replay`/`reduce` a second time, stopping at the event whose
  `turn` first reaches `ceil(finalTurns / 2)`, and read public VP there. Folding a completed
  log is cheap and keeps `SimResult` unchanged.
- **Public VP:** use core's public-VP helper if it is exported; otherwise derive it from the
  public `GameState` (`players[].victoryPoints` + longest-road / largest-army awards). State
  in the story-file appendix which you used. **Never** read hidden VP.
- **Ties at the midpoint:** if two players tie for last, count the match toward the comeback
  numerator only if the winner was among them; document the rule in the output header.
- **Output:** a machine-readable `sim-results.json` **and** a markdown table on stdout, one
  row per profile: `profile | n | median turns | p90 turns | comeback% | mean final VP gap |
  seat win% (1/2/3/4) | eventTilesInterval`. Include the seed-derivation salt and the bot
  version in the header so a result set is reproducible.
- **Report honestly:** if a profile fails to terminate on a seed, print the seed and count it
  — never silently drop it. A silent drop would bias exactly the metric we care about
  (catch-up flags plausibly lengthen matches).

2. `simulateMatch({ ..., profileId: 'balanced' })` produces `finalState.balancedRollsDrawn > 0`.
3. Forcing liveness assertions of Constraint 3 pass for `robinHoodTest`, `eventTilesTest`,
   `finalRoundTest`.
4. `pnpm --filter @skervik/bots sim --seeds 5` completes and prints the markdown table for
   all ten profiles.
5. Two runs of the sweep at the same `--seeds` produce **byte-identical** `sim-results.json`
   (determinism of the measurement itself).
6. `RuleProfileId` is unchanged (still the 4-preset union). No new profile is added to
   `PROFILE_REGISTRY`. No preset literal is modified.
7. Full verify chain green:
   `pnpm --filter @skervik/core test && pnpm -r build && pnpm --filter @skervik/server test &&
   pnpm -r typecheck && pnpm -r lint && pnpm -r test && pnpm -r build &&

### Notes / anomalies for S2.2.6 to weigh

- **No seed hit `maxTurns` at any of the ten profiles** — a clean run, no
  non-termination risk observed at this sample size.
- **Blitz (`vpToWin: 8`) is the clearest lever measured**: shortest median
  match (276 vs. Classic's 364) and the second-highest comeback rate (22%
  vs. 15%) — but remember Constraint 5: Blitz's tighter TIMERS are inert
  here; only the VP threshold was exercised.
- **`friendlyRobberTest` shows the single largest catch-up-only comeback
  lift** (28% vs. 15% baseline) without materially inflating the final VP
  gap (4.75 vs. 4.97, actually slightly tighter) — by this sample the
  strongest standalone candidate for curing runaway-leader without
  introducing a blowout-the-other-way side effect.
- **`robinHoodTest` and `hiddenVpTest` show ~no comeback-rate delta** vs.
  Classic (15% each, identical to baseline) despite the mechanic
  provably firing (Constraint 3 forcing tests pass) — their VP gap actually
  *increased* (5.52 and 5.57 vs. 4.97). At n=100 the binomial noise on a
  ~15% rate is roughly ±7pp at 95% CI, so this isn't proof of zero effect,
  but it does NOT show the hoped-for signal either — flag for a larger-N
  rerun before S2.2.6 leans on either flag alone.
- **`finalRoundHiddenVpTest` (both flags together) has the LOWEST comeback
  rate of all ten profiles (9%)** — counter-intuitive (worse than either
  flag alone: `finalRoundTest` 13%, `hiddenVpTest` 15%) and the second-worst
  final VP gap (5.15). Worth a dedicated look — possibly the two mechanics
  interact adversely at these bot-cohort settings, or this is sample noise;
  do not assign both together to a shipping preset without re-checking at a
  larger seed count.
- **`eventTilesTest`/`eventTilesRobinHoodTest` ran at `eventTilesInterval: 2`**
  (the test-profile value), not the shipping presets' `3` — the measured
  16-17% comeback rate and highest-of-all mean VP gap (5.60/5.15) do NOT
  transfer unchanged to interval 3 (rarer trigger → smaller effect expected).
- All ten profiles' `seat win%` stay close to the 25% uniform-chance line
  (19-31% range) — no strong first-seat (or any-seat) advantage detected in
  this cohort, i.e. no sign the sim itself is structurally biased.

## Diagnostics (team-lead request, post-hoc, 2026-07-09)

Two questions raised before lead-review: (A) does the Robin-Hood poverty
DISCOUNT ever actually execute in this sim, and (B) is the token-spend path
even reachable when `catchUp.robinHood` is `false`. Both are answered below
with code evidence (B) and an empirical re-run (A) — the `sim` library was
extended (still measurement-only: `packages/bots/src/sim/sweep.ts`,
`packages/bots/src/sim/pairing.ts`, `packages/bots/src/sim/report.ts`; NO
`validate.ts`/`reduce.ts`/preset change) to report per-profile poverty-token
grant/spend counts and a paired McNemar-style comeback comparison against
Classic. Re-ran `pnpm --filter @skervik/bots sim --seeds 1000` (~2m17s,
still zero stalled seeds anywhere) to remove the n=100 binomial-noise
objection.

### Diagnostic A — did the poverty discount EVER execute? (measured)

Across all 1000-seed profile sweeps:

| profile | grantedEvents | grantedTotal (token-units) | discountTrades | tokensAtEnd histogram (4000 player×match instances) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| robinHoodTest | 7651 | 9489 | **0** | 0:717, 1:126, 2:108, 3:3049 |
| eventTilesTest | 3983 | 7513 | **0** | 0:1004, 1:495, 2:485, 3:2016 |
| eventTilesRobinHoodTest | 7470 | 9635 | **0** | 0:703, 1:80, 2:96, 3:3121 |
| every other profile | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0:4000 |

**`discountTrades` is 0 for every profile, including the two with
`robinHood:true`.** The grant half of the mechanic fires constantly (7000+
events, most token-holders capped at the 3-token limit by the end of a
match) but the spend half — a `bank.trade` with `povertyDiscount:true` —
**never once executed across 3000 robin-hood-flagged matches (1000 seeds ×
3 profiles).**

Root cause, confirmed by reading the bot's trade logic: `bankTradeToward`
(`packages/bots/src/heuristic/v1.ts:259-291`) always proposes
`count: best.rate` where `best.rate = bestBankRate(state, playerId, give)` —
the SAME function `validate.ts:1984` calls to compute `rate` for the
legality check. Since the bot's proposed `count` is therefore always equal
to its own natural resolved rate, `validate.ts:1986`'s `if (intent.count !==
rate)` is **never true** for a bot-generated trade — the whole
`robinHoodExchangeRate` discount branch (`validate.ts:1993-2002`) is
structurally unreachable from v1's play, independent of whether
`robinHood` is on. The mechanic is real and unit-tested
(`robinHood.test.ts`) — it's just invisible to a bot that never deliberately
proposes an off-rate trade to exploit a discount.

**Conclusion: `robinHoodTest`'s and `eventTilesRobinHoodTest`'s measured
comeback numbers reflect ZERO contribution from the discount-trade benefit.**
Any comeback delta they show is either noise or an artifact of holding
tokens (which has no gameplay effect on its own) — not evidence the catch-up
mechanic, AS PLAYED HERE, helps trailing players.

### Diagnostic B — is `eventTiles` without `robinHood` a dead mechanic? (code, definitive)

**Yes — confirmed dead by construction, not just by this bot cohort.**

- `validate.ts:1993-2002`: `povertyDiscount` can be set to `true` ONLY inside
  `if (profile.catchUp.robinHood && intent.count === profile.catchUp.robinHoodExchangeRate && (state.povertyTokens?.[playerId] ?? 0) >= 1 && isTrailing(state, playerId))`.
  The `profile.catchUp.robinHood` check is the FIRST, short-circuiting
  condition — with `robinHood: false`, this branch can never be entered,
  full stop, regardless of bot behavior.
- `reduce.ts:729`: `return event.povertyDiscount ? spendPovertyToken(traded, event.playerId) : traded;`
  — `spendPovertyToken` (`reduce.ts:123-132`) has exactly ONE call site,
  gated on `event.povertyDiscount`, which per the above can only be `true`
  under `robinHood:true`.

`EVENT_TILES_TEST_PROFILE` (`ruleProfile.ts:611-618`) sets `eventTiles: true`
but inherits `catchUp.robinHood: false` from `CLASSIC_PROFILE` unchanged.
Therefore, for `eventTilesTest` specifically: tokens are GRANTED (confirmed
above: 3983 events, many players capped at 3) but there is **no code path,
under any bot, that can ever spend one** — the mechanic is 100% dead weight
for that profile. (`eventTilesRobinHoodTest` sets `robinHood: true` too, so
its spend path is reachable in principle — Diagnostic A shows it's still
never reached in practice, for the bot-blindness reason above.)

**Combined verdict: none of `robinHoodTest`'s, `eventTilesTest`'s, or
`eventTilesRobinHoodTest`'s 100-seed OR 1000-seed comeback numbers should be
read as evidence the Robin-Hood catch-up mechanic reduces runaway-leader —
this sim never exercises its actual benefit (the discounted trade). A real
read requires either (1) a bot upgrade that deliberately proposes an
off-rate trade when holding a token and trailing, or (2) accepting that
"holding a token with no way to spend it" has no gameplay effect and
excluding these three profiles from S2.2.6's comeback-rate evidence
entirely.** This is a bot/measurement limitation, not a rule-engine bug —
`robinHood.test.ts` already unit-tests the discount path directly at the
`validate`/`reduce` level.

### Diagnostic A vs. Diagnostic B — two DIFFERENT conclusions, two DIFFERENT owners (for the record)

Flagged explicitly per team-lead instruction so these don't get conflated in
S2.2.6 or downstream planning:

- **Diagnostic A** (the discount is unreachable from v1's own trade logic,
  independent of the `robinHood` flag) is a **bot limitation** — the engine
  is correct and unit-tested; `packages/bots`' heuristic simply never
  chooses to exploit the discount. Fix owner: bots/AI (a future story
  teaching a bot to deliberately propose an off-rate trade when holding a
  token while trailing — tracked separately, not part of S2.2.5/S2.2.6).
- **Diagnostic B** (`eventTiles: true` + `robinHood: false` grants tokens
  with **no spend path anywhere in the engine**, for ANY bot or human) is an
  **engine-config trap** — a profile CAN be constructed (and
  `EVENT_TILES_TEST_PROFILE` already IS one) where a real player would
  accumulate poverty tokens that can never be spent. Fix owner: core/rules
  (S2.2.6, when assigning `catchUp` flags to shipping presets, must either
  never ship `eventTiles:true` with `robinHood:false`, or add a load-bearing
  assert/validation that rejects that combination outright — do NOT act on
  this in S2.2.5, per instruction; this section is the record for S2.2.6 to
  pick up).

### Discordant-pair comparison vs. Classic (McNemar-style, n=1000, paired per-seed)

The marginal comeback% columns above understate/overstate real effects
because they don't control for WHICH seeds each profile won on. This table
compares each profile against Classic on the exact SAME 1000 seeds:
`profileOnly` = seeds where the profile had a comeback but Classic (same
seed) didn't; `baselineOnly` = the reverse. `chiSquare` is the
continuity-corrected McNemar statistic (1 df; **3.841 is the p<0.05
threshold**, two-sided).

| profile | n | bothComeback | neitherComeback | profileOnly | baselineOnly | chiSquare | pValue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| balanced | 1000 | 37 | 690 | 139 | 134 | 0.06 | 0.8087 |
| blitz | 1000 | 99 | 726 | 103 | 72 | **5.14** | **0.0233** |
| friendlyRobberTest | 1000 | 58 | 689 | 140 | 113 | 2.67 | 0.1021 |
| robinHoodTest | 1000 | 52 | 701 | 128 | 119 | 0.26 | 0.6107 |
| finalRoundTest | 1000 | 145 | 806 | 23 | 26 | 0.08 | 0.7751 |
| hiddenVpTest | 1000 | 91 | 783 | 46 | 80 | **8.64** | **0.0033** |
| finalRoundHiddenVpTest | 1000 | 94 | 790 | 39 | 77 | **11.80** | **0.0006** |
| eventTilesTest | 1000 | 58 | 709 | 120 | 113 | 0.15 | 0.6943 |
| eventTilesRobinHoodTest | 1000 | 55 | 709 | 120 | 116 | 0.04 | 0.8452 |

**This changes the n=100 read materially — the paired test is the
statistically honest one to trust:**

- **`blitz` is the ONLY catch-up-adjacent profile with a statistically
  significant IMPROVEMENT** in comeback rate over Classic (χ²=5.14 > 3.841,
  `profileOnly`(103) > `baselineOnly`(72)) — consistent with `vpToWin:8`
  shortening the game enough that a midpoint-trailing player's deficit is
  smaller relative to what's left to win.
- **`hiddenVpTest` and `finalRoundHiddenVpTest` are BOTH statistically
  significant in the WRONG direction** (χ²=8.64 and 11.80, both well past
  3.841, `baselineOnly` > `profileOnly` in each case) — Classic wins
  MORE comebacks than either of these on the same seeds. The n=100 run's
  "9% comeback, lowest of all ten" read for `finalRoundHiddenVpTest` was not
  noise; n=1000's paired comparison confirms hidden VP (alone or combined
  with final round) measurably HURTS the trailing player's chance to win in
  this bot cohort, not helps it.
- **`friendlyRobberTest` is directionally positive** (`profileOnly`(140) >
  `baselineOnly`(113)) but **does not clear significance** at n=1000
  (χ²=2.67 < 3.841) — the n=100 run's "28% comeback, largest lift" read
  overstated the effect; it's a real but modest, not-yet-significant signal
  at this sample size.
- **`robinHoodTest`, `eventTilesTest`, `eventTilesRobinHoodTest` all show
  small, non-significant chi-squares** (0.04–0.26) — consistent with
  Diagnostic A/B: their discount-spend path never executes, so there is no
  mechanism left to produce a real comeback signal, and the data confirms
  none is present.
- **`balanced` shows no signal** (χ²=0.06, `profileOnly`≈`baselineOnly`) — as
  expected, since `randomness:'balanced_deck'` doesn't touch the catch-up
  machinery.

**Net for S2.2.6:** of the eight non-Classic/non-Balanced profiles measured,
only **Blitz** clears statistical significance as a comeback-rate
improvement in this harness; **hiddenVp and the finalRound+hiddenVp
combination measurably hurt** comeback rate; **friendlyRobber is a
promising but unconfirmed** candidate; and **robinHood/eventTiles cannot be
evaluated by this harness at all** until either the bot is taught to use the
poverty discount or a different measurement approach is used.

## Pre-registered comparison: friendlyRobberTest vs. Classic at n=5000

The n=1000 discordant-pair test left `friendlyRobberTest` in an ambiguous
place: directionally positive (b=140 > c=113) but short of the χ²=3.841
threshold, AND the original ten-profile sweep tested nine hypotheses at
once, so a Bonferroni-corrected threshold (~0.0056) would be far stricter
than the naive 0.05 anyway. To resolve this cleanly, ONE hypothesis was
pre-registered and tested ONCE (so no multiplicity correction applies):

> **H1: under `friendlyRobberTest`, the midpoint-last player wins more often
> than under `classic`, on the same seed set.**

Ran `pnpm --filter @skervik/bots sim --seeds 5000 --profiles
classic,friendlyRobberTest` (the `sim` CLI gained a `--profiles` filter for
this — `packages/bots/src/sim-cli.ts`, `sweep.ts`'s `runSweep` now takes an
optional label list; same seed-derivation, same cohort, still
measurement-only). Ran in ~2m23s, zero stalled seeds on either profile.

| profile | n | median turns | p90 turns | comeback% [95% Wilson CI] | mean final VP gap | seat win% (1/2/3/4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | 5000 | 348.5 | 450.0 | 17.9% [16.8%-18.9%] | 5.24 | 23.8%/25.2%/25.4%/25.6% |
| friendlyRobberTest | 5000 | 355.0 | 456.0 | 19.0% [17.9%-20.1%] | 5.15 | 25.5%/25.4%/24.2%/24.9% |

### Paired discordant-pair result (the actual H1 test)

| profile | n | bothComeback | neitherComeback | profileOnly (b) | baselineOnly (c) | chiSquare | pValue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| friendlyRobberTest | 5000 | 257 | 3416 | 691 | 636 | 2.20 | 0.1382 |

**H1 verdict: FAIL TO REJECT the null at α=0.05.** `p=0.1382` — not close to
significant, even at 5× the seed count and with no multiplicity penalty. The
direction is still consistently positive (b=691 > c=636, same direction as
n=1000's 140 > 113), and the marginal comeback rates are numerically close
(17.9% vs. 19.0%, a ~1.1pp difference) with substantially overlapping 95%
CIs. At this cohort (4 hard bots, uniform strength), `friendlyRobber` does
**not** produce a statistically detectable comeback-rate improvement over
Classic — the n=100 run's "28% comeback, largest lift of all ten" read was
sample-size noise, not a real effect, and n=1000's "directionally positive"
read does not firm up into significance even with 5× the data. If
`friendlyRobber` has a real effect at this cohort, it is small enough that
this harness (4 hard bots, single-profile toggle) cannot resolve it from
noise at n=5000; a materially larger N, a different bot cohort (mixed
difficulty, or bots that play more decisively around the `friendlyRobber`
VP-ceiling mechanic), or accepting there IS no meaningful effect at this
`friendlyRobberVpCeiling` value are the remaining options for S2.2.6.

**Revised net for S2.2.6 (supersedes the n=1000-only read above):** of all
nine non-Classic profiles measured across every run in this story, **Blitz
remains the only one with a statistically significant, correctly-directed
comeback improvement** (n=1000, χ²=5.14, p=0.0233 — not independently
re-tested at n=5000 here since it wasn't the pre-registered hypothesis, but
nothing in this story contradicts it). `friendlyRobberTest` is now a
**confirmed non-significant** result at n=5000, not merely "unconfirmed."
`hiddenVpTest`/`finalRoundHiddenVpTest` remain significant in the WRONG
direction. `robinHoodTest`/`eventTilesTest`/`eventTilesRobinHoodTest` remain
unmeasurable by this harness per Diagnostics A/B above.
