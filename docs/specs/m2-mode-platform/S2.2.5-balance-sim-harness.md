# S2.2.5 — Balance-simulation harness

## Why this story exists

E2.2 shipped four catch-up mechanics — friendly robber, robin-hood / poverty tokens,
final round + hidden VP, catch-up event tiles. **Every one of them is `false` on every
shipping preset.** `BALANCED_PROFILE` is literally `{...CLASSIC_PROFILE, randomness:
'balanced_deck'}` (`ruleProfile.ts:399-404`); `BLITZ_PROFILE` is Classic with `vpToWin: 8`
and tighter timers (`ruleProfile.ts:413-429`).

The M2 gate says "Classic / Balanced / Blitz all playable". Before **S2.5.4 (Lobby UI)**
lets a human select a profile, we must decide **what each preset actually is**. That
decision is a product decision, and the owner will make it (S2.2.6) — but it must be made
on **evidence**, not taste. This story produces the evidence.

**This story changes NO preset and NO rule.** It only makes it possible to *measure* them.

## Goal

`@skervik/bots` can simulate bot-vs-bot matches under **any registered rule profile**, and
a sweep script reports, per profile: **match length**, **comeback rate**, **final VP gap**,
and **per-seat win rate**.

## The three metrics (and why each)

| Metric | Definition | What decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| **Match length** | `SimResult.turns` — distribution (median, p90, max) | Does Blitz's `vpToWin: 8` actually shorten a match, or is the ≤60-min ceiling held only by timers? Feeds S2.1.3's adaptive calculator. |
| **Comeback rate** | ~~Share of matches where the player **last by public VP at the midpoint turn** wins~~ **SUPERSEDED (S2.2.5a)** — this definition is the confound. Now: share of matches where the winner was **trailing at the anchor turn `T*`** (first turn the leader reaches `ceil(V/2)` public VP), *trailing* = at least `ceil(V/4)` public VP behind the leader. Both cuts scale with `V = vpToWin`. | Whether catch-up cures *runaway leader*. Classic is the baseline; each flag is a **delta** against it. **A between-profile delta is only valid when both arms are scored at the SAME cuts** — see `## H2′ / H3′`. |
| **Final VP gap** | `winnerVP − lastPlaceVP` at `phase:'finished'` (public VP) | Guards against a catch-up flag that raises comeback rate by inflating variance (a blowout in the other direction is not an improvement). |
| **Per-seat win rate** | Win share by seat index | Detects first-player advantage; also a sanity check that the sim isn't biased. Not a preset lever — a validity check on the sim itself. |

**Public VP only, everywhere.** Under `hiddenVp` the public value is what a player can
actually see, so measuring on public VP keeps the metric comparable across profiles. Never
read hidden VP for these metrics.

## Hard constraints (the landmines)

1. **Classic must stay byte-frozen.** `reduce.ts:143-172` sets `state.profileId` *only if
   the `match.started` event carries it* — precisely so pre-S2.1.1 golden fixtures replay
   identically. Therefore, when the sim runs Classic, `match.started` must be emitted
   **without a `profileId` key** (not `profileId: 'classic'`, not `profileId: undefined`).
   Otherwise the event bytes shift.
   *Forcing check:* a test asserts `'profileId' in matchStartedEvent === false` for the
   default/Classic path, and `=== true` for a non-Classic profile.

2. **The existing seed-sweep must stay green, unchanged.** `harness.test.ts:109-153`
   (24 seeds × 3 difficulties) is the no-stall gate. It must pass without edits — proving
   the `profileId` param is genuinely optional and Classic behavior is untouched.

3. **The flag must actually fire.** A measured "no effect" is worthless if the mechanic
   never triggered. *Forcing check:* over the `robinHoodTest` sweep, assert at least one
   `poverty.tokensGranted` event was emitted; over `eventTilesTest`, assert
   `finalState.sevensRolled` is defined and > 0; over `finalRoundTest`, assert at least one
   `game.finalRoundStarted`; over `balanced`, assert `finalState.balancedRollsDrawn > 0`.
   These four assertions are what separate a real measurement from a green no-op.

4. **Determinism / paired comparison.** Bot seeds are derived per `(difficulty, matchSeed,
   playerId)` (`harness.ts`) and are **independent of the profile**. Keep it that way: the
   same match seed must produce the same bot noise across all ten profiles, so profile
   deltas are paired, not confounded by resampled bot behavior. The sim must never feed the
   match seed to a bot (`Bot.decide` takes exactly `(state, playerId)` — `bot.ts:20-25`).

5. **Timers are inert in the sim.** `simulateMatch` caps by action count (`maxTurns`,
   default 6000), not wall clock. Blitz's tighter timers therefore have **zero** effect
   here; only `vpToWin: 8` is measurable. The report must state this explicitly — otherwise
   a reader will conclude Blitz's timer settings were validated when they were not.

6. **No wall-clock, no ambient RNG anywhere near core.** The sim lives in `bots`; core stays
   pure. `check-core-no-runtime-deps.mjs` must stay green.

7. The sweep is **long-running — it must NOT join `pnpm -r test`.** It is a `sim` script.
   Only the small forcing tests (Constraints 1–3) run in the test suite, at low seed counts.

## Sim design

- **Fixed cohort:** 4 players, all seats `difficulty: 'hard'`. Uniform strength isolates the
  *rule's* effect from bot-skill asymmetry. (Mixed-difficulty cohorts are a later question;
  do not add them now.)
- **Seeds:** default 100 per profile, `--seeds N` overridable. Derive them deterministically
  (the `deriveValue('sweep-salt', i)` pattern at `harness.test.ts:113-125`) so any run is
  reproducible and every profile sees the **same seed set**.
- **Midpoint VP:** `SimResult` exposes only `finalState` + `events` (`harness.ts:29-34`) —
  no per-turn snapshots. Do **not** extend `SimResult` with snapshots. Instead fold the
  returned `events` with core's `replay`/`reduce` a second time, stopping at the event whose
  `turn` first reaches `ceil(finalTurns / 2)`, and read public VP there. Folding a completed
  log is cheap and keeps `SimResult` unchanged.
- **Public VP:** use core's public-VP helper if it is exported; otherwise derive it from the
  public `GameState` (`players[].victoryPoints` + longest-road / largest-army awards). State
  in the story-file appendix which you used. **Never** read hidden VP.
- **Ties at the midpoint:** if two players tie for last, count the match toward the comeback
  numerator only if the winner was among them; document the rule in the output header.
- **Output:** a machine-readable `sim-results.json` **and** a markdown table on stdout, one
  row per profile: `profile | n | median turns | p90 turns | comeback% | mean final VP gap |
  seat win% (1/2/3/4) | eventTilesInterval`. Include the seed-derivation salt and the bot
  version in the header so a result set is reproducible.
- **Report honestly:** if a profile fails to terminate on a seed, print the seed and count it
  — never silently drop it. A silent drop would bias exactly the metric we care about
  (catch-up flags plausibly lengthen matches).

## Retraction (S2.2.5a, 2026-07-09) — READ BEFORE LIFTING ANY NUMBER BELOW

> **Every comeback number produced under the seed salt `balance-sim-salt` is
> void, and every conclusion drawn from one is withdrawn.** The engineering was
> clean; the *metric* was confounded with the treatment variable. The root cause
> was the story spec (the tie rule was written into it verbatim), not the
> implementation. The corrected metric, the confound audit that would have caught
> it, and the re-run live in `S2.2.5a-fix-comeback-metric.md` and in the
> `## H2′ / H3′` section at the end of this file.

**The defect.** `metrics.ts`'s `lastPlacePlayers` returned *every* player tied at
the minimum midpoint public VP, and `sweep.ts` scored a comeback whenever the
winner was among them. So the comeback probability scaled with the **size of the
midpoint tie-set**: a three-way tie scored a "comeback" 75% of the time under a
uniformly random winner, and a four-way tie scored 100% — describing a match with
*no leader to come back from*.

**Why that destroys H2/H3 specifically.** The old midpoint was *endogenous*:
`ceil(finalTurn/2)`, half the **realized** match length. Lowering `vpToWin`
shortens the match → moves the midpoint earlier → fewer VP accrued by then →
**more ties** → a higher "comeback" rate, with no change whatsoever in catch-up
dynamics. The metric moved with the treatment.

`lead-review` replicated the harness independently (same salt, same bot
derivation; their Classic n=1000 rate reproduced ours exactly at 17.1%) and
stratified by tie-set size:

| trailing-set size | classic | blitz | `__vp9_test__` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (sole last) | 7.1% | 7.1% | 6.5% |
| 2 | 20.8% | 20.2% | 21.2% |
| 3 | 51.7% | 45.2% | 44.5% |
| 4 | 100% | 100% | 100% |

Within every stratum the profiles are **indistinguishable**. Only the stratum
*weights* move (mean tie-set size: classic 1.576, blitz 1.775). Tie-free paired
McNemar collapses the reported effects to nothing:

| comparison | reported χ² | tie-free χ² |
|---|---|---|
| blitz vs classic | 61.81 | **0.04** |
| `__vp9_test__` vs classic | 17.14 | **0.21** |
| hiddenVp vs classic | 8.64 | **0.03** |

**Retracted claims** (each is struck at its site below, and none may be cited):

1. `## Pre-registered comparison H2` — "**H2 verdict: SUPPORTED**". Void.
2. `## Pre-registered comparison H3` — "**H3 verdict: SUPPORTED**". Void.
3. "Net for S2.2.6: H3 is a confirmed … comeback improvement." Void — and the
   product decision it drove (Balanced → `vpToWin: 9`) **has been revoked by the
   owner**. `BALANCED_PROFILE.victory.vpToWin` stays `10`.
4. `hiddenVp` / `finalRound`+`hiddenVp` "measurably **HURT** comeback". Void.

**A second, independent error, also retracted.** The framing "17.9% vs the 25% a
random winner would give" was presented as *the measured size of runaway-leader*.
It is not. **25% is the win rate of a _random_ player, not of a _trailing_ one.**
A player who is behind should win *less* than chance in any game with skill or
accumulation — that is what "behind" means. The comparison never had a null to
compare against. Every occurrence of that framing is removed. The corrected
metric reports `P(win | trailing)` over player-slots instead, which *does* have
~0.25 as its symmetry null.

## Pre-registration for the S2.2.5a re-run (written BEFORE the re-run was executed)

Recorded in commit `805de01`, *before* any H2′/H3′ number existed. The corrected
metric's cuts are `anchorVp = ceil(V/2)` and `deficitThreshold = ceil(V/4)`,
owner-approved. I did **not** tune them. But they carry a residual asymmetry that
must be on record before the results, not after:

| profile | V | anchor `ceil(V/2)` | as % of V | deficit `ceil(V/4)` | as % of V |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| blitz | 8 | 4 | 50.0% | 2 | 25.0% |
| `__vp9_test__` | 9 | 5 | 55.6% | 3 | 33.3% |
| classic | 10 | 5 | 50.0% | 3 | 30.0% |

**Why I did not "fix" this.** Victory points are integers, and `V/4` is
`2 / 2.25 / 2.5` for `V = 8 / 9 / 10`. **No integer threshold keeps the ratio
constant across these three profiles** — rounding is unavoidable, so this is a
property of the measurement problem, not a bug in the choice of `ceil`. Changing
it could only trade one rounding artifact for another, and doing so *after*
seeing a result is exactly the failure this story exists to correct.

**Two consequences, predicted in advance:**

1. **H2′ (blitz) is _not_ fully controlled.** The bots are `vpToWin`-blind
   (nothing outside `sim/` reads it) and Blitz's timers are inert in the sim, so
   on a shared seed `classic` / `blitz` / `__vp9_test__` produce a **byte-identical
   event prefix** until the first player reaches 8 total VP. Blitz's anchor
   therefore fires at a strictly *earlier point in the same game* (leader at 4
   public VP, not 5). The absolute trailing cutoff happens to coincide — blitz
   trails at `publicVp <= 4-2 = 2`, classic at `<= 5-3 = 2` — but it is *measured
   earlier*, when fewer VP have accrued, so **more players sit at or below it**.
   Blitz's trailing-count weights are biased **upward** under the null.
   → **A higher marginal comeback rate for blitz is EXPECTED under the null.** The
   marginal H2′ McNemar is therefore *not interpretable on its own*. A null for
   H2′ is a **strong, conservative** null. A *positive* H2′ would be suspect and
   may be reported only if the §2 audit shows the within-stratum **rates** moved,
   not the weights.

2. **H3′ (vp9) _is_ near-perfectly controlled** — and this doubles as a falsifiable
   check on my own implementation. `anchorThresholds(9)` is identical to
   `anchorThresholds(10)` (anchor 5, deficit ≥3). Combined with the identical
   event prefix, `classic` and `__vp9_test__` must reach the **same `T*` with the
   same trailing set on the same seed**, differing only in *who wins* (vp9 stops
   at 9 total VP). **Prediction: their `trailingCountHistogram`, `medianAnchorTurn`
   and `anchorMissingMatches` are identical**, up to the rare seeds where the
   anchor is reached only after a player has already banked ≥4 hidden VP cards.
   If those columns differ materially, **the metric code is wrong** and the H3′
   number must not be reported. H3′ thus isolates the one causal question cleanly:
   *conditional on the same trailing set, does stopping the race at 9 instead of 10
   change whether a trailing player wins?*

Multiplicity: two pre-registered hypotheses → **Bonferroni, α = 0.025 each**
(two-sided), i.e. reject only at χ² ≥ 5.024 (1 df).

> `finalRoundHiddenVpTest`'s "lowest of all ten, 9%" — are all products of the

- **Blitz (`vpToWin: 8`) is the clearest lever measured**: shortest median

- **`friendlyRobberTest` shows the single largest catch-up-only comeback

structurally unreachable from v1's play, independent of whether
`robinHood` is on. The mechanic is real and unit-tested
(`robinHood.test.ts`) — it's just invisible to a bot that never deliberately
proposes an off-rate trade to exploit a discount.

comeback numbers reflect ZERO contribution from the discount-trade benefit.**
Any comeback delta they show is either noise or an artifact of holding
tokens (which has no gameplay effect on its own) — not evidence the catch-up
mechanic, AS PLAYED HERE, helps trailing players.

### Diagnostic B — is `eventTiles` without `robinHood` a dead mechanic? (code, definitive)

**Yes — confirmed dead by construction, not just by this bot cohort.**

### Exploratory: is match LENGTH (not the VP threshold per se) the operative variable?

**Labelled exploratory — no p-value verdict attached to this subsection.**
Within each profile's own 5000-match sweep, matches were split at that
profile's own median `turns` into a shorter half and a longer half, and the
comeback rate was measured in each half separately (`sim/lengthSplit.ts`,
already wired into `buildReport`'s `lengthSplits` field and the CLI's
markdown output — no new statistic, a pure fold over `turnsBySeed` +
`comebackBySeed`).

| profile | shorterN | shorter comeback% | longerN | longer comeback% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | 2500 | 14.6% | 2500 | 21.1% |
| blitz | 2504 | 21.2% | 2496 | 24.4% |

**This runs the OPPOSITE direction from the "shorter match, less room for a
leader's advantage to compound" mechanism.** Within both profiles, the
LONGER half of matches shows the HIGHER comeback rate, not the shorter half
— Classic's shorter half (14.6%) is actually below Classic's own overall
rate (17.9%), and nowhere close to Blitz's overall rate (22.8%); Classic's
two halves also don't "look the same" (14.6% vs. 21.1%, a 6.5pp gap) — so
neither of the two diagnostic patterns proposed in the task ("short Classic ≈
Blitz overall" or "Classic's halves look the same") is what the data shows.

**The confound flagged up front is almost certainly why, and it should be
read as dominating this split rather than as background noise.** Matches are
not short at random: a runaway leader who is far enough ahead finishes the
game (reaches `vpToWin`) in fewer turns, which mechanically produces a short
match that is *also* a non-comeback by construction — the leader who ends
the game quickly is, almost tautologically, the same player who was already
ahead and stayed ahead. That selection effect alone would push the
"shorter half" comeback rate down independent of any true causal effect of
duration on catch-up dynamics, and the observed direction (shorter → lower
comeback, in both profiles) is exactly what that selection effect predicts.

**Net reading:** this observational, non-randomized split cannot support
"match length is the operative variable" behind Blitz's H2 result — if
anything, the within-profile correlation runs backward from that hypothesis,
most plausibly because the runaway-leader-ends-early confound swamps
whatever length effect might exist. This does not rule the length mechanism
out (a randomized truncation experiment, not an observational split, would
be needed to test it cleanly) — it just means this particular measurement
provides no evidence for it, and offers a plausible alternative explanation
(selection bias in which matches are short) for why the naive within-profile
correlation looks the way it does.

**What H2 establishes, and what it does not.** H2 (above) establishes that
lowering `vpToWin` (Classic 10 → Blitz 8) improves the comeback rate — that
result is a confirmed, paired, statistically significant comparison. H2 does
**not** establish *why* a lower threshold helps. Two distinct mechanisms
remain live and this story does not — and, given only an observational split
of a single confounded variable, cannot — adjudicate between them:

1. **Less time to compound:** a shorter match gives a leader's advantage
   fewer turns to compound before the game ends.
2. **Less distance to close:** a lower absolute VP target means a
   midpoint-trailing player has fewer points left to make up, independent of
   how many turns that takes.

Both are consistent with H2's result on their own; this story's evidence
does not distinguish them, and no claim in this document should be read as
favoring one over the other. Disentangling them would need a design this
harness does not have — e.g. holding turn count fixed while varying
`vpToWin`, or a randomized truncation experiment — not the descriptive split
run here.

# H2′ / H3′ — the re-run under the corrected metric (S2.2.5a, 2026-07-09)

Fresh seed salt **`balance-sim-salt-v2`** (rotated in `805de01`: a hypothesis may
not be re-tested on the same data after the metric that produced its result was
changed to fix that result). `--seeds 5000`, 4 `hard` bots per seat, one shared
seed set across all arms. **Every number below is reproducible from committed
code** by the exact command that precedes it. Two runs at the same salt +
`--seeds` produce byte-identical JSON (verified).

**Multiplicity, fixed in advance:** two pre-registered hypotheses → Bonferroni,
**α = 0.025 each** (two-sided) → reject only at **χ² ≥ 5.024** (1 df).

## The one thing that decides every number here: WHERE the instrument is placed

The corrected anchor `T*` is the first turn at which the leader reaches
`ceil(V/2)` public VP. `ceil(V/2)` is **4** for blitz and **5** for classic.

The v1 bot is **`vpToWin`-blind** (nothing outside `sim/` reads it) and Blitz's
timers are inert in the sim, so on a shared seed `classic`, `blitz` and
`__vp9_test__` produce a **byte-identical event prefix** until someone wins.
An arm scored at *its own* cuts is therefore measured **at a different point of
the very same game**. That is not a comeback effect. It is an instrument
position.

So the harness now makes the cuts an **explicit, matched input** (`0c09d67`):

- `runSweep` scores **every arm at the `classic` baseline's cuts by default.**
- Letting an arm score itself is opt-in (`--self-scored`).
- `computeAllDiscordantPairs` **throws** if an arm's `scoredAtVpToWin` differs
  from the baseline's, unless the caller passes `allowMismatchedCuts`.

Both runs below are shown, because the difference between them *is* the finding.

## What this harness can and cannot measure

**The most durable thing this story produces.** Every number above is bounded by
it, and none of the retractions would have been necessary if it had been written
down first.

**It CAN measure mechanics that operate through accumulation and stopping rules** —
things that change the arithmetic of a game the agents are already playing:
`vpToWin`, board length, the VP value of awards, the shape of the randomness
(`balanced_deck`).

**It CANNOT measure any mechanic whose effect runs through how players choose to
behave.** The v1 bot has a fixed policy. It does not fear the robber, cannot be
embarrassed, does not negotiate, and does not change plan when the finish line
moves. Three of this epic's flags land squarely in that blind spot:

- **`friendlyRobber` is a _feel_ rule.** Its value is that being robbed at 2 VP is
  demoralising. A bot has no morale. (H1: null — and that null means little.)
- **`hiddenVp` is a _social_ rule.** Its value is that opponents cannot see who is
  about to win and so cannot coordinate against them. Our bots never coordinate.
- **`robinHood` is an _agency_ rule.** Its value is a choice about when to spend a
  poverty token. The v1 bot never spends one: **0 discount trades in 3000 matches.**
  We measured a mechanic our agents cannot execute.

**And it bounds the `vpToWin` result specifically.** The bot is `vpToWin`-blind.
That is precisely *why* Classic and Blitz share a byte-identical event prefix, and
therefore *why* the matched-cut contrast is so clean — but it means we measured
**exactly one channel: "the same game, stopped earlier."** That channel
mechanically favours whoever is ahead when the music stops, so of course a shorter
race yields fewer comebacks. **A human at `vpToWin: 8` plays a different game:
they race.** They take the greedy opening, skip the long road, and buy the
development card that ends it. Whether *that* game has more or fewer comebacks is
**invisible to this harness at any sample size**, because no amount of seeds will
make a non-adaptive agent adapt.

So the honest statement of the dose-response is not "Blitz is bad for comebacks."
It is: **"holding strategy fixed, stopping the race earlier lets the leader keep a
lead they already had."** That is a fact about the stopping rule, not about the
mode. Measuring the mode requires agents that respond to `vpToWin` — a bot
capability, not a bigger sweep.

**Rule of thumb for anyone extending this harness:** before you sweep a flag, ask
what a *player* does differently because of it. If the answer involves fear,
negotiation, coordination, deception, or a choice this bot cannot make, the sim
will return a confident null and it will mean nothing.

## Consequence for S2.1.3 (adaptive duration) — a finding, not a fix

`computeAdaptiveDuration` (S2.1.3) lowers `vpToWin` to fit the 60-minute ceiling.
The dose-response says that, **through the stopping-rule channel**, this is not a
free action: it **trades comeback rate for duration.**

| `vpToWin` | median turns | comeback rate |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 347 | 14.6% |
| 9 | 309 | 13.2% |
| 8 | 271 | 11.3% |

Shortening the match works — reliably, and that is the metric-independent half of
the result. But each point of `vpToWin` surrendered to the clock costs roughly
1.5pp of comeback rate under non-adaptive agents, and runaway-leader is one of the
two chronic pains this product exists to design out. **S2.1.3 and S2.5.4 should
weigh that tradeoff explicitly rather than treating a lower VP threshold as a pure
duration knob.**

Two caveats bound this, both from the section above: the effect is measured in the
stopping-rule channel only (a human would re-plan), and Blitz's real-time levers —
its tighter turn timers — are inert in an action-capped sim, so Blitz-the-preset
was never measured, only `vpToWin: 8`. **`computeAdaptiveDuration` is NOT touched
by this story** (out of scope, Law 3); this is recorded for whoever owns it next.

## Net for S2.2.6 (supersedes both earlier "Net" paragraphs)

1. **No catch-up flag has a demonstrated comeback effect in this harness**, in
   either direction — and for `friendlyRobber`, `hiddenVp` and `robinHood` the
   harness is the wrong instrument, not merely an underpowered one.
2. **Lowering `vpToWin` does not improve comeback rate. It reduces it** — cleanly,
   monotonically, significantly (V=8: 11.3%, V=9: 13.2%, V=10: 14.6%), *in the
   stopping-rule channel*. Any future argument for a lower threshold must rest on
   **match duration**, which it genuinely delivers.
3. `BALANCED_PROFILE.victory.vpToWin` **stays 10.** The decision to lower it was
   revoked by the owner and is now also contradicted by the corrected measurement.
4. The `__vp9_test__` / `__balanced_vp9_test__` fixtures were required for this
   re-run and are retained; **their removal moves to S2.2.6.**
5. Any future paired comparison must run at **matched cuts** (the default) and must
   state the channel it measured. The harness now enforces the first and documents
   the second.
