# S2.2.5a — Fix the comeback metric

## What went wrong

`lead-review` BLOCKED the S2.2.5 merge. The engineering is clean; **the metric is confounded
with the treatment variable.**

`metrics.ts:56-63` — `lastPlacePlayers` returns **every** player tied at the minimum midpoint
public VP. `sweep.ts:182-187` — `isComeback = trailing.includes(winnerId)`.

So the comeback probability scales with the size of the midpoint tie-set. A three-way tie for
last scores a "comeback" 75% of the time under a uniformly random winner; a four-way tie scores
100% — describing a match with no leader to come back from.

And **lowering `vpToWin` enlarges the tie-set**: shorter match → earlier midpoint (it is
`ceil(finalTurn/2)`) → fewer VP accrued → more ties. The metric rises with no change whatsoever
in catch-up dynamics.

The reviewer replicated the harness independently (same salt, same bot derivation; their Classic
n=1000 rate reproduced ours exactly, 17.1%) and showed:

| trailing-set size | classic | blitz | `__vp9_test__` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (sole last) | 7.1% | 7.1% | 6.5% |
| 2 | 20.8% | 20.2% | 21.2% |
| 3 | 51.7% | 45.2% | 44.5% |
| 4 | 100% | 100% | 100% |

Within every stratum the profiles are indistinguishable. Only the weights move (mean tie-set
size: classic 1.576, blitz 1.775). Tie-free paired McNemar: blitz **χ²=0.04** (reported: 61.81),
vp9 **χ²=0.21** (reported: 17.14), hiddenVp **χ²=0.03** (reported: 8.64).

**H2, H3 and the hiddenVp-harms-comeback finding are all void.** So is the product decision they
drove (Balanced → `vpToWin: 9`), which the owner has revoked.

A second, subtler error in the original spec, also to be corrected: "17.9% vs the 25% a random
winner would give" was presented as the measured size of runaway-leader. It is not. 25% is the
win rate of a *random* player, not of a *trailing* one. A player who is behind should win less
than chance in any game with skill or accumulation — that is what "behind" means.

The root cause is the Queen's spec (the tie rule was written into it verbatim), not the workers'
implementation.

## Goal

A comeback metric that is **invariant to `vpToWin`**, a re-run of H2/H3 on a **fresh seed set**
as newly pre-registered hypotheses, an honest story file, and the reviewer's five secondary
findings closed.

## 1. The corrected metric (owner-approved design)

Both the anchor and the deficit threshold must scale with the VP threshold, so neither moves
when the treatment moves.

**Anchor (exogenous, replaces `ceil(finalTurn/2)`):** let `V = loadRuleProfile(state.profileId ??
'classic').victory.vpToWin`. `T*` is the first `GameState.turn` at which the leading player's
**public** VP reaches `ceil(V/2)`. The old midpoint was *endogenous* — half the **realized**
match length, a quantity the outcome itself determines.

**Trailing (strict deficit, size-invariant):** at `T*`, with leader public VP = `L`, player `p`
is trailing iff `L - publicVp(p) >= ceil(V/4)`. Ties for last are no longer a numerator: a
four-way tie has nobody trailing.

**Primary outcome (per match, binary):** the winner was trailing at `T*`. Paired McNemar across
profiles on a shared seed set, exactly as before.

**Sensitivity outcome (the reviewer's cheap option):** unique last place at the OLD midpoint;
ties dropped from the denominator, drop count reported. If primary and sensitivity agree, the
conclusion is robust. If they diverge, the anchor still leaks — report that, do not pick the
one you like.

Public VP only, always — one swept profile is `hiddenVp`. Keep using `computePublicVictoryPoints`.

## 2. MANDATORY confound audit — run and report BEFORE any headline number

This is the operationalised lesson. A new metric ships with its own audit or it does not ship.

For every profile, report:
1. the distribution of the **number of trailing players** at `T*` (0,1,2,3);
2. the comeback rate **within each stratum** of that count;
3. the count of matches with **zero** trailing players (excluded from the primary numerator by
   construction — the winner cannot have been trailing);
4. `T*` itself: median and p90, per profile.

**If the within-stratum rates agree across profiles and only the stratum weights differ, you have
reproduced the artifact in a new coat. Say so plainly and report NO headline verdict.** That
outcome is a success for this story, not a failure.

Also report, as a descriptive companion, the conditional `P(win | trailing)` computed over
*player-slots* rather than matches (denominator = every (match, player) pair where the player was
trailing at `T*`). Under a null of "trailing doesn't matter" this equals ~0.25 by symmetry, which
makes it directly interpretable — unlike the old marginal rate.

## 3. Re-run H2 and H3 as NEW pre-registered hypotheses, on a FRESH seed set

The old H2/H3 are burnt: a hypothesis may not be re-tested on the same data after the metric was
changed to fix a result. **Change the seed-derivation salt** (e.g. `balance-sim-salt-v2`), state
the new salt in the story file, and re-run.

> **H2′:** under `blitz` (`vpToWin: 8`), the winner was trailing at `T*` more often than under
> `classic` (`vpToWin: 10`), on the same (new) seed set.
> **H3′:** the same, for `__vp9_test__` (`vpToWin: 9`) vs `classic`.

Two hypotheses → **correct for two comparisons** (Bonferroni α = 0.025 each) or say explicitly
that you did not. Run at `--seeds 5000`. Report b, c, bothComeback, neitherComeback, McNemar χ²
(continuity-corrected), two-sided p, marginals with 95% Wilson CIs, median/p90 turns, mean final
VP gap — plus the §2 audit for each profile.

**Report whatever comes out.** A null result here is the expected outcome given the reviewer's
tie-free analysis, and it is the correct answer. Do not tune the deficit threshold `ceil(V/4)`
until something becomes significant — that is the same failure one level up. If you believe the
threshold is wrong, say why *before* looking at the result, and change it once, documented.

Also re-run, with the corrected metric on the new salt, the descriptive numbers for
`hiddenVpTest` and `finalRoundHiddenVpTest` — the "hiddenVp measurably HURTS" claim must be
replaced by whatever the corrected metric actually shows (the reviewer's tie-free χ²=0.03
suggests: nothing).

## Findings

**The spec's anchor is still confounded for a between-profile contrast, and this
story's own data proves it.** §1 claims a lower-`vpToWin` profile "reaches the
anchor at a proportionally equivalent point." It does not. `anchorVp = ceil(V/2)`
is 4 for blitz and 5 for classic, so on a byte-identical event prefix blitz's `T*`
lands at turn 28 where classic's lands at 45. The deficit cut moves by a *different*
ratio (`ceil(V/4)` = 25% of V for blitz, 30% for classic), so the two do not cancel.
Measuring earlier means fewer VP have accrued, so more players sit below the cutoff:
blitz's `k=3` stratum holds 2235 matches where Classic's holds 769.

The fix removed the original *endogeneity* (the anchor no longer depends on the
realized match length) but not *cross-profile incomparability*. **A between-profile
comeback contrast is valid only when both arms are scored at the same cuts.**
Because `anchorSnapshot(events, ids, vpToWin)` takes `vpToWin` as a free parameter,
this costs nothing to do: re-scoring blitz at classic's cuts (anchor 5, deficit ≥3)
yields trailing sets identical to classic's on **4996/4996** seeds, and flips the
result from "blitz +20pp comeback (χ²=627)" to "blitz *fewer* comebacks (χ²=68)".

The §2 audit as designed would NOT have caught this on its own: it tests for
"identical within-stratum rates, different weights", and here the within-stratum
rates differ too — because for blitz the stratum labels denote different game
situations (`k=3` = "someone ran away" in Classic, "it is early" in blitz). The
audit was still what surfaced the problem, via the `T*` median column.

Recommendation for S2.2.6: make the baseline's `vpToWin` an explicit parameter of
any paired comparison, rather than letting each arm score itself.

**Threshold discipline.** `ceil(V/4)` was **not** tuned. Argued *before* looking at
any outcome (`8c5fe94`) that integer VP make exact proportionality impossible
(`V/4` = 2 / 2.25 / 2.5), so rounding is a property of the problem, not a knob. The
one methodological change made after seeing data — scoring both arms at matched cuts
— was pre-argued in that same commit, is a controlled contrast rather than a
threshold, and moves the headline **against** the hypothesis.

**Decisions.** `comebackRate`'s denominator is `completed - anchorMissing`, not
`completed`: a match whose leader never showed `ceil(V/2)` public VP has no anchor,
so it is counted and excluded rather than folded into the numerator (only
`__vp9_test__` has any: 2). Zero-trailing matches stay in the denominator and can
never be in the numerator. `trailingCountHistogram` is pre-seeded `0..3` in fixed key
order so `JSON.stringify` output cannot depend on which stratum a run observes first
(determinism AC).

**Surprising.** The `__vp9_test__` vs `classic` contrast is *naturally* controlled —
`ceil(9/2) == ceil(10/2)` and `ceil(9/4) == ceil(10/4)` — so, the bots being
`vpToWin`-blind, the two arms share a byte-identical prefix and trailing set on every
seed, differing only in who wins. That was predicted in advance and then confirmed at
4996/4996, which validates the metric implementation itself. It also means the two
controlled results line up monotonically (V=8: 11.3%, V=9: 13.2%, V=10: 14.6%) — a
clean dose-response in the direction *opposite* to the retracted H2/H3.

**Sensitivity outcome is structurally invalid** and cannot arbitrate: it conditions on
tie-freeness at an endogenous midpoint, a post-treatment variable the treatment moves
(blitz drops 2925 matches, classic 2233), so pairing on "non-null in both arms" selects
a treatment-dependent subsample. Reported anyway, as specified; its null reproduces
`lead-review`'s tie-free numbers, which is a useful cross-implementation check.
