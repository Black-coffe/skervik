# S2.4.4 — Teach the v1 bot to spend a poverty token

## Why

S2.2.5 Diagnostic A: across 3000 matches with `robinHood: true`, the discounted bank trade
executed **zero times**. Not once.

`bankTradeToward` (`packages/bots/src/heuristic/v1.ts:259-291`) always proposes
`count: best.rate` where `best.rate = bestBankRate(state, playerId, give)` — the *same function*
`validate.ts:1984` calls for the legality check. So `validate.ts:1986`'s `if (intent.count !== rate)`
is never true for a bot-proposed trade, and the entire discount branch (`validate.ts:1993-2002`)
is structurally unreachable. The mechanic's granting half fired constantly (7651 events); its
paying-out half never ran.

Two consequences, and the second is the one that matters today:

1. **`robinHood` and `eventTiles` are not measured, they are unmeasurable-by-this-agent.** We
   cannot say "no effect"; we can only say "our instrument cannot execute the mechanic."
2. **Bots sit at real tables.** S2.3.3 fills an abandoned seat with a bot. In any profile with
   `robinHood` enabled, that bot plays strictly worse than a competent human, because it cannot
   use the one rule written specifically for the player who is behind. This is a live quality
   defect, not a hypothetical.

## Goal

The v1 bot proposes a discounted bank trade when — and only when — the engine would accept one.
Then re-measure `robinHood` with the mechanic actually exercised.

## Scope — exact files

**`packages/core`** (single export, zero behavior change):
1. `src/validate.ts` — `isTrailing(state, playerId)` (`~:471-475`) already exists. Export it.
2. `src/index.ts` — re-export it.

**Do not let the bot re-implement "trailing."** If the bot's notion diverges from `validate`'s by
so much as a tie-break, it will propose trades the server rejects, and we will have replaced a
silent no-op with a silent rejection. One definition, one source.

**`packages/bots`**:
3. `src/eval/features.ts` — a new pure feature, e.g. `povertyDiscountRate(state, playerId, give):
   number | null`, returning the discounted rate when a discounted trade is legal for this player
   and this resource, else `null`. **Put the decision in `eval/`, not in the decision loop** — M4's
   assist-mode advisor consumes the same evaluation to hint "you can trade 2:1 right now" (plan §3,
   design-for-reuse). Bot and advisor share one brain.
4. `src/heuristic/v1.ts` — `bankTradeToward` proposes `count: discountRate` when the feature
   returns one, else `count: best.rate` as today.

**Touch nothing else.** No `reduce.ts`, no rule change, no protocol, no server, no client.

## The legality condition — mirror `validate`, do not paraphrase it

A discounted trade is accepted iff (`validate.ts:1993-2002`):

```
profile.catchUp.robinHood
  && intent.count === profile.catchUp.robinHoodExchangeRate
  && (state.povertyTokens?.[playerId] ?? 0) >= 1
  && isTrailing(state, playerId)
```

Two things the naive reading misses:

- **`validate.ts:1986` only reaches the discount branch when `intent.count !== bestBankRate`.** If
  the player holds a 2:1 port for that resource, `bestBankRate === 2 === robinHoodExchangeRate`,
  the normal path handles it, and **no token is spent**. So the bot must propose the discount only
  when `robinHoodExchangeRate < bestBankRate(state, playerId, give)` — otherwise it either changes
  nothing or (worse) burns a token for a rate it already had. G3 (S2.2.6) guarantees
  `robinHoodExchangeRate < bankTrade.baseRate`, but says nothing about a *port* rate.
- **A token is a finite, capped resource** (`robinHoodTokenCap`, default 3). Spending it on a
  trade the bot would have made anyway at 3:1 is worse than saving it. Decide deliberately whether
  the bot spends greedily (whenever legal and strictly cheaper) or holds. **Greedy is the correct
  v1 answer** — it is simple, it is what the mechanic is for, and a hoarding heuristic is a
  speculative optimisation (Law 2). Say so in the story file; do not build the hoarding version.

## Hard constraints (the landmines)

1. **The bot must stay `vpToWin`-blind.** This is not stylistic. `classic`/`blitz`/`__vp9_test__`
   produce byte-identical event prefixes *because* the bot never reads `profile.victory`, and the
   entire matched-cut contrast (S2.2.5a) rests on that. Reading `profile.catchUp` is fine — it is
   `false` on all three of those profiles. **Never read `profile.victory` in the bot brain.**
   *Forcing check:* the existing sim must still show identical trailing-set histograms and `T*`
   medians across `classic`/`blitz`/`__vp9_test__` under matched cuts.
2. **Classic byte-frozen.** `robinHood` is `false` on every shipping preset, so this change must be
   a **no-op on every shipping preset**. `harness.test.ts`'s 24-seed × 3-difficulty sweep must pass
   **unmodified**, and the v0 brain (`heuristic/v0.ts`) must not be touched.
3. **The bot never reads the match seed.** `Bot.decide(state, playerId)` — two params
   (`bot.ts:20-25`). Unchanged.
4. **`isTrailing` reads public VP only** (`computePublicVictoryPoints`). Confirm it does. The bot
   must not gain a hidden-VP read through this export — `hiddenVp`'s unmeasurability rests on the
   bot never seeing hidden state, and `docs/wiki/rule-profiles.md` now says so in print.
5. Core stays runtime-dep-free.

## Forcing tests — bind the *outcome*, not the trigger

This story exists because a green forcing test once certified that a token was **granted**. Do not
repeat that. The mechanic's outcome is a **spend**.

1. A unit test: a trailing player holding ≥1 token, under a `robinHood` profile, with a natural
   rate of 4:1 or 3:1 → the bot proposes `count === robinHoodExchangeRate`.
2. A unit test: the same player holding a **2:1 port** for that resource → the bot proposes the
   port rate and **no token is spent** (the discount would buy nothing).
3. A unit test: a **non-trailing** token-holder → the bot proposes its natural rate.
4. A unit test: `robinHood: false` → the bot proposes its natural rate, byte-identically to today.
5. **An integration test over `simulateMatch` on `robinHoodTest`: at least one `bank.trade` event
   carries `povertyDiscount: true`.** This is the one that would have caught the original defect,
   and it must fail against the current bot. **Verify that it does, and say so in your report.**

### Constraint 4 — `isTrailing` reads public VP only (verified at the line, before export)

`validate.ts`'s `isTrailing` (S2.2.2, `~:471-477`) computes
`computePublicVictoryPoints(state, playerId) <= leaderPublicVp(state) - catchUp.robinHoodVpGap`.
`leaderPublicVp` itself folds `computePublicVictoryPoints` over every player. Both call sites are
`computePublicVictoryPoints` — the SAME public-VP helper `isStealable` (S2.2.1) uses, which
deliberately omits held VP dev cards (`validate.ts:406-431`'s docstring). No path through
`isTrailing`, and therefore no path through the export or `povertyDiscountRate`, reads
`state.devCards` or any other hidden field. The export does not hand the bot a hidden-VP read;
`hiddenVp`'s unmeasurability-by-the-bot claim is unaffected.

### Poverty tokens have two emitters; the feature keys on the SPEND gate only

`poverty.tokensGranted` is emitted from two sites: the storm grant (`validate.ts:1167`, gated on
`catchUp.eventTiles`) and the non-7-roll production grant (`validate.ts:1200`, gated on
`catchUp.robinHood`). `povertyDiscountRate` reads neither emission site — it reads only
`state.povertyTokens?.[playerId]` (a plain count, source-blind) and gates on
`profile.catchUp.robinHood` alone, exactly matching the spend legality at `validate.ts:1993-2002`.
A token granted by a storm under `eventTilesRobinHoodTest` (where both flags are `true`) is just as
spendable as one granted by the production path under `robinHoodTest` (where only `robinHood` is
`true`) — the feature cannot distinguish them and does not try to.

### `bankTradeToward`: module-private → exported (scope note)

`heuristic/v1.ts`'s `bankTradeToward` was changed from module-private to `export`ed. This is a
real, if small, surface-area change beyond the story's literal file list, made so its behavior
(which discount rate it proposes, for which `give`/`get` pair) is directly unit-testable without
depending on `mainCandidates` happening to rank a bank trade as the top-scored candidate in some
constructed state. No behavior changed by the export itself — flagged here so the reviewer doesn't
have to infer it from the diff.

### Forcing test 5 — method and number, against the UNMODIFIED bot

Before any source change, `v1.robinHoodIntegration.test.ts` was written importing only symbols
that already existed pre-S2.4.4 (`simulateMatch`, `createHeuristicBot`, `EXPERIMENTAL_PROFILE_IDS`,
the S2.2.5 sweep seed/cohort helpers) — no dependency on `povertyDiscountRate` or the exported
`bankTradeToward`, so it could run against the pre-fix bot unmodified. `git stash` set aside the
`features.ts`/`v1.ts` edits; the test then ran a 30-seed × 4-hard-bot sweep under `robinHoodTest`
and asserted at least one `bank.trade` event carries `povertyDiscount: true`. Result: **0 of 30
seeds produced a discounted trade — the assertion failed**, reproducing the S2.2.5 Diagnostic A
defect on fresh seeds. `git stash pop` restored the fix; the identical test then passed. This
demonstrates the test binds the *spend* outcome, not merely the *grant* trigger the original
(defective) forcing test certified.

### H4 results (`--seeds 5000`, salt `balance-sim-salt-v2`, 4 hard bots, `pnpm --filter @skervik/bots sim -- --seeds 5000 --profiles classic,robinHoodTest,eventTilesRobinHoodTest`)

`robinHoodTest` and `classic` are both `vpToWin: 10`; the sweep confirms rather than assumes this —
all three arms report `scored at = 10`, `anchor VP = 5`, `deficit >= 3` (identical numbers), so the
matched-cut machinery is a no-op here and the contrast is clean by construction. Unlike the
classic-vs-blitz check above, the arms' *events* are not expected to be byte-identical: `robinHood`
changes bot behavior (it now spends tokens), so `T*` itself can legitimately differ a little — and
it does (classic 45.0 / robinHoodTest 46.0 / eventTilesRobinHoodTest 45.0 median; p90 69.0 for all
three). That small shift is part of the measured effect, not a cuts artifact.

| robinHoodTest | 46.0 | 69.0 | 1046 | 1786 | 1445 | 723 | 1046 |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | 6.4% | 20.0% | 41.9% | 10.4% [9.7–11.1%] |

**The audit does NOT reproduce the S2.2.5a artifact shape.** In that defect, within-stratum rates
*agreed* across arms and only the stratum *weights* differed. Here both move: `robinHoodTest` has
MORE zero-trailing matches than `classic` (1046 vs 965 — catch-up is reducing how often anyone
ends up trailing at all) **and** higher within-stratum win rates at every stratum (+2.3pp / +2.4pp
/ +3.1pp at k=1/2/3). Two independent channels moving the same direction is evidence of a real
effect, not a re-weighting artifact — so a verdict may be drawn.

**Poverty-token diagnostics** — the mechanic that was previously unmeasurable now runs at scale:

|---|---|---|---|---|

**H4 headline (primary outcome, McNemar vs. `classic`, single pre-registered comparison — no
multiplicity correction):**

| profile | n | b (profileOnly) | c (baselineOnly) | χ² | p (two-sided) | comeback% [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| robinHoodTest | 5000 | 542 | 470 | 4.98 | 0.0256 | 16.1% [15.1–17.1%] vs classic 14.6% [13.7–15.6%] |

Median turns 350.0 vs classic's 347.0 (p90 449.0 vs 446.0 — matches run marginally longer, as
expected: catch-up narrows gaps rather than ending the race sooner). Mean final VP gap 4.85 vs
classic's 5.26 (narrower spread, consistent direction).

**Sensitivity outcome** (old endogenous anchor, ties dropped — S2.2.5a already established this
check is *structurally invalid* as an arbiter, since it conditions on tie-freeness, a
post-treatment variable the treatment itself moves; reported per spec, not treated as a tiebreaker):
robinHoodTest χ²=3.37, p=0.066 (n=1819, b=149, c=118) — same direction as primary (more comebacks
under robinHood) but does not independently cross p<0.05.

**H4 verdict: weakly, marginally supported.** The primary outcome is nominally significant at
α=0.05 (p=0.0256) with the hypothesized direction (robinHood → more comebacks), corroborated by
two audit-level signals moving the same way (fewer zero-trailing matches, higher within-stratum win
rates) and by the mean-VP-gap narrowing. The effect size is modest (+1.5pp marginal, roughly
+2–3pp per stratum) and the sensitivity check — while not a valid independent arbiter — sits just
above the conventional threshold rather than clearly corroborating. This is real signal, not the
S2.2.5a artifact shape, but it is a small effect, not a strong one. Per instruction, the bot is
**not** being tuned in response to this number.

**`eventTilesRobinHoodTest` — descriptive only, no verdict against Classic (exploratory, would be a
second bite at the same apple):** comeback 16.1% [15.1–17.1%], within-stratum rates and audit shape
closely track `robinHoodTest`'s (rate@1 8.5%, @2 21.8%, @3 45.9%; 1034 zero-trailing), mean VP gap
4.87, 66179 discount trades across 5000 matches. Runs at `eventTilesInterval:2` (the test-profile
value; shipping presets would use `interval:3`, so this number does not transfer unchanged — noted
by the sim's own report header).

No stalled seeds in any of the three arms across 5000 seeds each.
